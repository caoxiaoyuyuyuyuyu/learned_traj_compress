\section{Distillation Attempt and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:distillation}

Phase~1 localised a descriptive weight-geometry correlate and a negative activation-probing result. We now test causal interventions to identify where the failure actually lies. We establish DPO and capacity baselines (\S\ref{sec:dpo_results}), examine the truncation--behavioral-mismatch link (\S\ref{sec:suppression}), and test the causal hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:causal}).

%--------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:distill_design}

\paragraph{Training pipeline.}
We train a pooled SFT baseline and three per-$N$ DPO variants on Qwen2.5-3B (LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} rank 32). SFT uses 1{,}118 partial-credit trajectories (pooled $N\!\in\!\{2,4,8\}$; $\text{total\_em}\!\geq\!1$) for 3 epochs at $\text{lr}\!=\!2\!\times\!10^{-4}$. Each DPO variant initialises from the SFT checkpoint, trains on $N$-specific preference pairs matched to $176$ raw pairs at $\beta\!=\!0.05$, $\text{lr}\!=\!2\!\times\!10^{-5}$, 1 epoch. Full hyperparameters in Appendix~\ref{app:training}.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Models are evaluated on held-out splits (hash-based, $\text{test\_frac}=0.25$) for $N\!\in\!\{2,4,8\}$ with $\text{max\_new\_tokens}=4096$ ($251/131/125$ prompts). We report em\_partial, em\_full, and truncated\_rate; significance via paired bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples); pre-registered practical-significance threshold $\Delta\text{em\_partial}\!\geq\!+5$pp at $N\!=\!8$. The matched-sample constraint subsamples DPO to the $N\!=\!4$ bottleneck of 176 raw pairs---a structural consequence of multi-objective collapse rather than a remediable sampling issue (Appendix~\ref{app:dpo}).

\paragraph{Metric: action-sequence accuracy.}
Let $\mathbf{s}=(s_1,\dots,s_m)$ and $\mathbf{t}=(t_1,\dots,t_n)$ denote the ordered action-tag sequences extracted from student and teacher trajectories respectively, where each tag $\in\{\texttt{think},\texttt{search},\texttt{information},\texttt{answer}\}$. Action-sequence accuracy is defined as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asa}
\text{ASA}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t})=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\min(m,n)}\mathbf{1}[s_i=t_i] & n>0,\\
\mathbf{1}[m=0] & n=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The denominator is the \emph{teacher} sequence length, so a student that emits a correct prefix but fewer actions is penalised proportionally. In the \texttt{honest\_search} setting both students deterministically emit the 3-token prefix $\langle\texttt{think},\texttt{search},\texttt{information}\rangle$ on every sample; the resulting score therefore reduces to $3/n$ and is driven entirely by teacher sequence length, not by student-side variation (per-sample forensics in Appendix~\ref{app:asa_forensics}).

%--------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{DPO and Capacity Ablations}
\label{sec:dpo_results}

We also evaluate DPO on matched preference pairs as a stronger-than-SFT baseline; the detailed analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:dpo}) confirms DPO yields only $+1.0$pp ($p=0.163$, power $>0.99$ at 5pp), and does not close the gap at the 176-pair matched-sample scale (power excludes effects $\geq$ 5pp but remains scale-limited for smaller improvements).

\paragraph{Capacity isolation.}
\label{sec:capacity}
A 7B full-rank pooled SFT (same data/inclusion as the 3B LoRA student) consistently outperforms the 3B student ($0.171$/$0.147$/$0.079$ vs.\ $0.112$/$0.084$/$0.025$ at $N\!=\!2$/$4$/$8$). To avoid denominator confounding, we report all components in absolute pp at $N{=}8$: the retrieval-quality residual contributes $\approx 11$\,pp (oracle\_redacted\_full $-$ honest\_search baseline); capacity contributes $\approx 5$--$6$\,pp (derived from the 7B--3B absolute gap $0.079-0.025=0.054$); and the leakage component contributes $\approx 37$\,pp and remains the dominant term. Defining the capacity contribution in relative terms as $({\text{em}_{7\text{B}} - \text{em}_{3\text{B}}}) / ({\text{em}_{\text{teacher}} - \text{em}_{3\text{B}}})$, the 7B--3B gap accounts for \emph{at most} $12.3$/$13.8$/$12.6\%$ of the teacher--student gap---a stable $\approx$12--14\% upper bound (the 7B uses full-rank fine-tuning, more expressive than rank-32 LoRA, so the training-method advantage is conflated in). This is an upper bound on capacity alone. Crucially, the retrieval-quality bound must now be computed against the \emph{leakage-free} component of oracle recovery, not the full leakage-inclusive $+47.7$pp: using the passage-redacted oracle (\S\ref{sec:causal}, exp\_016) as the denominator, the non-leakage retrieval-quality upper bound is $\text{em}_{\text{oracle\_redacted\_full}} - \text{em}_{\text{honest\_search}} = 0.131 - 0.021 = 0.110$ at $N\!=\!8$, against which the $\approx$12--14\% capacity bound is no longer a trivial fraction. We therefore interpret capacity as accounting for a non-negligible share of the retrieval-quality-adjusted gap (on the order of comparable magnitude to the retrieval-quality component itself), while passage-level answer-leakage explains the remaining ${\sim}37$pp of the naive oracle gain.

%--------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Truncation and Behavioral Mismatch}
\label{sec:suppression}

We identified and controlled for a truncation artefact that inflated early DPO signal: without retrieval, both students hallucinate \texttt{<search>}/\texttt{<information>} segments that consume $>80\%$ of the generation budget, causing $\geq 83\%$ output truncation at $N\!=\!8$ (truncation scales monotonically as $30.7\%\!\to\!55.7\%\!\to\!83.2\%$ for $N\!=\!2/4/8$, with DPO showing the same pattern). A suppression ablation (Appendix~\ref{app:trunc_confound}) confirms truncation is the surface manifestation, not the root cause, of behavioral mismatch.

%--------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Causal Verification: Oracle Retrieval}
\label{sec:causal}

If the failure stems from executing search procedures without a retrieval backend, providing the backend should recover performance. We run two interventions: (i)~\textbf{BM25 retrieval}, a realistic deployable baseline (our lead comparison, \S~\emph{Realistic retrieval: BM25}, Table~\ref{tab:bm25}), which replaces the missing backend with a lexical retriever over the teacher's training document pool; and (ii)~\textbf{oracle (teacher-retrieval replay)}, an upper-bound probe that intercepts the student's \texttt{<search>} outputs at inference time and injects the passages the RL teacher actually retrieved on the same prompt. We present the oracle result first because it establishes the maximum recoverable headroom; BM25 (Table~\ref{tab:bm25}) then quantifies how much of that headroom a realistic system recovers without any answer-adjacent passages. Oracle substantially exceeds BM25 ($+47.7$pp vs $+12.6$pp at $N{=}8$); the redaction ablation below isolates how much of the oracle--BM25 gap is retrieval quality vs passage-level substring leakage.

Table~\ref{tab:oracle_api} shows oracle retrieval transforms student performance: em\_partial jumps $0.021\!\to\!0.498$ at $N\!=\!8$ ($+47.7$pp, $p<0.0001$, 95\% CI $[44.6,50.9]$pp); truncation drops $83.2\%\!\to\!0\%$. This provides a \emph{leakage-inclusive upper-bound} on retrieval-only mitigation: the intervention is necessary but not sufficient evidence for a pure retrieval-quality bottleneck, because teacher-replayed passages also contain gold-answer substrings (Appendix~\ref{app:oracle_impl}). We note here that ``oracle'' is used throughout for brevity; the intervention is technically a \textit{teacher-retrieval replay} (we replay the teacher's actual retrieval output without any answer-key oracle).

\paragraph{Answer Leakage Audit and Passage-Redaction Ablation.}
We audit potential answer leakage in teacher information segments. On 50 random $N{=}8$ evaluation prompts (seed=42, lowercased substring match), 98\% of teacher \texttt{<information>} blocks contain at least one gold-answer substring; on a derangement-shuffled control (teacher passages from unrelated prompts), the rate is 34\% (a 64\,pp real--shuffled gap that quantifies retrieval topic specificity). The absolute 98\% rate motivates a direct causal test: \textbf{passage-redaction} (exp\_016). On the full phase1d\_v2 $N{=}8$ test split (125 prompts), we re-evaluate the SFT student with oracle-replay under three redaction conditions---$0\%$ (\texttt{oracle\_full}, sanity-gated to within $\pm0.02$ of Table~\ref{tab:oracle_api}), $\sim\!50\%$ per-passage density (\texttt{oracle\_redacted\_half},
uniform density halving applied to all $125/125$ oracle passages;
\texttt{redact\_info\_block\_tag} produces $867$ substitutions vs $1663$
in the full condition, ratio $0.52$; see Appendix~\ref{app:redaction}), and $100\%$ (\texttt{oracle\_redacted\_full})---replacing gold-answer substrings with a \texttt{[REDACTED]} placeholder under a word-boundary regex (see Appendix~\ref{app:redaction} for protocol and a pre-registered deviation log). Results (Table~\ref{tab:redaction_dose_response}, Figure~\ref{fig:redaction_dose_response}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item em\_partial ($N{=}8$): $0.021$ (\texttt{honest\_search}) $\to 0.131$ (\texttt{oracle\_redacted\_full}) $\to 0.373$ (\texttt{oracle\_redacted\_half}) $\to 0.498$ (\texttt{oracle\_full}). The three intermediate points are strictly monotone ($0.131 < 0.373 < 0.498$), confirming a dose-response relationship in the redaction dimension.
  \item $\Delta_{\text{full\_redact}} = +11.0$\,pp [$+8.8, +13.2$] (paired bootstrap 10k resamples, seed 42, vs \texttt{honest\_search}; $p < 0.0001$). This \emph{leakage-free} residual is the retrieval-quality contribution.
  \item $\Delta_{\text{half\_redact}} = +35.2$\,pp [$+32.2, +38.2$]; $\Delta_{\text{oracle\_full}} = +47.7$\,pp [$+44.6, +50.9$].
  \item Decomposition: retrieval-quality contributes $\approx 11$\,pp ($\approx 23\%$ of oracle gain); passage-level answer-leakage-assisted decoding contributes $\approx 36.7$\,pp $[+33.5, +40.0]$ ($\approx 77\%$; paired bootstrap 10k resamples, seed 42, on \texttt{oracle\_full} $-$ \texttt{oracle\_redacted\_full}, $p < 0.0001$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Span-importance distribution.}
Halving the per-passage substitution density (from $1663$ to $867$ total \texttt{[REDACTED]} substitutions across all $125/125$ passages, ratio $0.52$ via the preprocessing-level \texttt{redact\_info\_block\_tag} tool) retains $73.8\%$ of the oracle gain ($0.352 / 0.477$). The first half of substitutions per passage therefore contributes approximately $74\%$ of the leakage mechanism, while the second half contributes only $\approx 26\%$. This pattern is \emph{consistent with but not proving} a non-uniform distribution of span importance: because the halving selection rule inherits from the \texttt{redact\_info\_block\_tag} tool's internal deterministic ordering, we cannot attribute the $74\%$-vs-$50\%$ gap to span-level information content without further ablation. The effect is nevertheless less extreme than a uniform-span model would predict (where halving density should yield $\approx 50\%$ recovery, not $74\%$). The span-importance asymmetry is itself an upper bound (word-boundary substring redaction removes exact-match but not paraphrase hints).

We also note that the $+11.0$\,pp retrieval residual is a \emph{lower bound} on pure retrieval contribution: a residual leakage probe (Appendix~\ref{app:redaction_limitations}) finds that $\sim\!60\%$ of user-question blocks retain partial disjunctive answer content because the multi-hop question templates enumerate candidate answers by structural necessity. A \texttt{question\_redacted} counterfactual (not run here) would be needed to isolate pure retrieval contribution from structural question-side leakage; between-condition deltas are symmetric because all three oracle conditions share the same user-question block, so our retrieval-quality estimate is unaffected in \emph{delta} even though absolute leakage floors are overestimated.

This is a \emph{partial-mechanism} verdict by our pre-registered gates (\S3.5 decision table, plan.md \S6): retrieval execution is necessary (the non-zero retrieval-quality residual is statistically and practically significant, $+11$\,pp is larger than both the DPO effect and the capacity-isolation effect of the 7B SFT) but not sufficient (the majority of the oracle gain evaporates once passage-level answer substrings are masked). The orthogonal checks from the v3.4 audit still apply as \emph{lower bounds} on mechanism separation: (i) the teacher (MEM1) was trained with PPO outcome rewards only, with no gold-label supervision of the retriever; (ii) student $\mathrm{em\_full}$ at $N{=}8$ remains $0.008$ under teacher-retrieval replay, three orders of magnitude below the ${\sim}1.0$ expected if passages directly exposed answers as full strings. The $77\%$ leakage component therefore reflects \emph{substring-level answer exposure enabling short-form decoding}, not full-answer copy-paste, and is itself an upper bound on leakage (word-boundary substring redaction removes exact-match but not paraphrase hints).

\paragraph{Realistic retrieval: BM25.}
Replacing oracle with BM25 recovers $14$--$26\%$ of the oracle gain: em\_partial improves $0.021\!\to\!0.147$ at $N\!=\!8$ ($+12.6$pp; Table~\ref{tab:bm25}, Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval_recovery}), with truncation dropping to $0\%$ across all $N$. The BM25 index is built from the \emph{Doc chunks extracted from the teacher's own \texttt{<information>} blocks} across all training trajectories ($N\!\in\!\{2,4,8\}$), i.e., the passages the FlashRAG-backed teacher retriever actually returned during RL rollouts. This index therefore lies within the distribution of documents the teacher saw during RL training rather than a held-out Wikipedia dump, so the $+12.6$pp recovery is an upper bound on what a BM25 deployment can achieve over this exact document pool (not a generalisation-to-new-corpus bound). The BM25--oracle gap reflects retrieval quality, not student capability: the student's queries match the teacher's (Jaccard $=0.95$).

\paragraph{Query fidelity.}
The student averages exactly $1.0$ search per prompt across 507 test cases---matching the teacher's $1.04/1.01/1.06$ at $N\!=\!2/4/8$ (the prompt prefix already includes the first retrieval result)---and its queries match the teacher's at mean Jaccard $0.95$ (median $1.0$). SFT faithfully transfers both the search pattern and the query formulation; the bottleneck is purely the absence of a retrieval backend. Despite recovering em\_partial to near-teacher levels, em\_full remains low ($0.008$ at $N\!=\!8$) due to per-objective $\approx$50\% independence stacking ($0.5^N\!\approx\!0.4\%$ at $N\!=\!8$).

\subsection{Synthesis}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The evidence converges on three points: (i)~\textbf{preference learning leaves the SVD alignment ratio unchanged}---an observational correlate that does not drive protocol decisions; DPO remains within $1$pp of SFT at $N\!=\!8$ despite richer training signal, against the $2$--$6\!\times$ vs.\ $1.47\!\times$ supra-random alignment gap (\S\ref{sec:framework}); the causal root of the em\_partial gap is isolated by the oracle retrieval intervention (\S\ref{sec:causal}), not by this geometric observation; (ii)~\textbf{the student imitates a strategy it can partially execute but not exploit}---oracle retrieval recovers em\_partial $0.021\!\to\!0.498$ at $N\!=\!8$ ($+47.7$pp, $p<0.0001$; \S\ref{sec:causal}), but a passage-redaction ablation attributes only ${\sim}11$pp of this to retrieval quality and ${\sim}37$pp to passage-level answer-leakage-assisted decoding. Retrieval execution is therefore \emph{necessary but not sufficient}: the student's queries match the teacher's (Jaccard $0.95$) and the retrieval backend reliably surfaces topic-relevant passages, but the student cannot fully leverage those passages in the absence of near-substring answer hints. The teacher--student gap then has two distinct sub-failures: (a) retrieval precision (closed by any real retrieval backend, $+11$\,pp headroom) and (b) student-side passage utilisation without substring hints (the remaining $+37$\,pp, not closed by retrieval alone). (iii)~\textbf{truncation distorts the comparison}---after controlling for budget, the DPO advantage shrinks from $+2.4$pp ($p<0.001$) to $+1.0$pp ($p=0.163$). At most $12$--$14\%$ of the gap is attributable to capacity (\S\ref{sec:capacity}); this remains an independent upper bound alongside the retrieval-quality ($\sim 11$\,pp) and leakage ($\sim 37$\,pp) components (Figure~\ref{fig:gap_decomposition}).

Table~\ref{tab:cross_backbone} shows every diagnostic signal reappears on Llama-1B: (i)~truncation scales monotonically ($29.9\%\!\to\!51.2\%\!\to\!85.6\%$ vs.\ Qwen's $30.7\%\!\to\!55.7\%\!\to\!83.2\%$); (ii)~oracle retrieval drops truncation to $0\%$ at every $N$ with a $+33.7$pp gain at $N\!=\!8$ (vs.\ $+47.7$pp for Qwen, both large and monotonic in $N$); (iii)~BM25 partially recovers performance and also collapses truncation. Surface metrics at $N\!=\!8$ are backbone-agnostic to within noise: tool-call syntax $=1.00$, balanced XML $=1.00$, non-empty queries $=1.00$, BLEU $=1.16$, ROUGE-L $=0.21$ (Appendix~\ref{app:silent_failure_full}). Action-sequence accuracy $=0.606$ for both students, but per-sample forensics (Appendix~\ref{app:asa_forensics}) reveals this coincidence is structurally degenerate: both backbones emit the identical 3-token action prefix on every sample, so the score reduces to $3/n_\text{teacher}$. This metric therefore reflects a shared action-prefix pattern, not an independently diagnostic signal of silent failure. The transferable lesson is the protocol, not the specific 3B model it was developed on; the remaining surface estimates (BLEU, ROUGE-L) show overlapping bootstrap CIs at floor level (Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}).
