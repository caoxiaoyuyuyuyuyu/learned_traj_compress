\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope and capacity isolation.}
All experiments use one RL teacher (MEM1, 7B) on one domain (multi-objective RAG QA). Student-side we cover two backbones and two scales (Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.2-1B, cross-family, ${\sim}3\times$ size difference) and observe the same silent-failure signature on both (\S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}); direct replication on other teachers and benchmarks remains future work. The 7B full-rank pooled SFT quantifies the capacity contribution at $\approx$12--14\% of the teacher--student gap (\S\ref{sec:capacity}), but the full-rank vs.\ LoRA training-method mismatch makes this a best-effort upper bound.

\paragraph{DPO data scarcity and retrieval oracle.}
DPO uses only 176 matched pairs ($0.4\%$ perfect rate at $N\!=\!8$), a structural consequence of multi-objective collapse, not a remediable sampling issue; power exceeds $0.99$ for a 5pp effect (floor 2.7pp). Oracle retrieval uses teacher passages and is thus an upper bound, but BM25 retrieval confirms the finding generalizes beyond the oracle setting ($+6$--$12$pp within the teacher's training document pool, truncation $\to 0\%$).

\paragraph{Paraphrase-level leakage floor (not ablated).}
Our passage-redaction ablation (\S\ref{sec:causal}, Appendix~\ref{app:redaction}) uses word-boundary substring matching on gold-answer strings. It therefore removes exact lexical exposure but leaves an irreducible \emph{paraphrase-level} leakage floor---e.g., ``Einstein's theory'' surviving after ``Albert Einstein'' is redacted, or semantic rewordings that encode the answer without matching any gold substring. A paraphrase-aware or entailment-aware redactor was considered but not run in this paper (Appendix~\ref{app:redaction_limitations}). Consequently, the reported $+11$\,pp retrieval-quality residual is itself an upper bound on the non-leakage mechanism, and the $+37$\,pp leakage component is a lower bound; tightening these would require a held-out paraphrase-redacted replay that we flag as a direct follow-up.

\paragraph{Question-side residual leakage.}
\texttt{oracle\_redacted\_full} redacts passage \texttt{<information>} blocks but not user-question blocks, and a residual leakage probe (Appendix~\ref{app:redaction_limitations}) finds that $\sim\!60\%$ ($75/125$) of user-question blocks at $N\!=\!8$ still expose partial disjunctive answer content because the multi-hop questions enumerate candidates by structural necessity. Between-condition deltas are symmetric (the three oracle conditions share the same user-question block), so the $+11.0$\,pp retrieval residual is unaffected, but absolute leakage floors are overestimated by this structural source. A \texttt{question\_redacted} counterfactual condition (not run in this paper) would be needed to isolate pure retrieval contribution from structural question-side leakage, and we flag it as a sensitivity direction for future work.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

\paragraph{Data.} All training data comes from FlashRAG (MIT license)~\cite{jin2025flashrag} and the MEM1 teacher trajectories (MIT license)~\cite{zhou2025mem1}. The underlying QA datasets (NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, and FlashRAG's multi-hop mixture) contain no personally identifiable information beyond what is already public on Wikipedia.

\paragraph{Computational cost and environmental impact.} Our experiments used approximately 50 GPU-hours on an NVIDIA RTX~PRO~6000 Blackwell (97\,GB, $\sim$600\,W TDP) single-node setup, distributed across Qwen2.5-3B / 7B / Llama-3.2-1B SFT, DPO, and evaluation runs (the 7B full-rank SFT and the Qwen-3B eval sweep dominate the budget). Using the carbon-estimation methodology of Strubell et al.\ (2019) with an average draw of $\sim$0.5\,kWh per GPU-hour and an estimated grid carbon intensity of $\sim$0.5\,kg\,CO$_2$/kWh, this corresponds to roughly $12$\,kg\,CO$_2$-equivalent. No experiments were rerun for cosmetic reasons; rerun counts are bounded by the registry entries summarised in Appendix~\ref{app:training} and~\ref{app:llama1b_hparams}.

\paragraph{Misuse considerations.} Our diagnostic framework exposes that format-level metrics alone can mask complete task failure in agentic distillation. Deployed systems relying on tool-call format validators as safety filters may therefore be vulnerable to silent-failure modes in which the student produces syntactically well-formed tool calls that never reach a valid retrieval backend. We recommend content-level evaluation and end-to-end tool-execution ablations as complementary checks for any distilled agent.

\paragraph{Bias and limitations.} Our evaluation is English-only and Wikipedia-centric, inheriting the coverage bias of the FlashRAG corpus. The silent-failure mode we document may manifest differently in multilingual, domain-specific, or non-retrieval agentic settings. All students are trained under a single hash-based train/test split; we did not vary the split seed (see \S\ref{sec:limitations}).
