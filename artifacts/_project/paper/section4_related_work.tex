\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{RL-trained LLM agents.}
RL fine-tuning endows language models with procedural capabilities that SFT does not produce. MEM1~\cite{zhou2025mem1}, WebArena~\cite{zhou2024webarena}, AgentBench~\cite{liu2024agentbench}, ReAct~\cite{yao2023react}, and AgentTuning~\cite{zeng2024agenttuning} all demonstrate multi-step action strategies for tasks ranging from QA to web navigation. We investigate why these RL-acquired strategies resist distillation.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation for LLMs.}
Standard methods~\cite{hsieh2023distilling,gu2024minillm,jiang2023lion} target single-turn transfer on static inputs. Policy distillation in RL~\cite{rusu2016policy,czarnecki2019distilling} and interactive imitation learning (DAgger;~\citealp{ross2011dagger}) transfer procedural knowledge but in low-dimensional action spaces. Our setting differs: the teacher's competence arises from a \emph{multi-turn procedural policy} in the high-dimensional token space of LLMs.

\paragraph{SFT--RL alignment gaps and weight-space analysis.}
The ``alignment tax''~\cite{askell2021alignment} documents RLHF degrading pre-training capabilities. \citet{jin2025rl} show SFT induces ``hard alignment'' while RL produces ``soft alignment''; \citet{aghajanyan2021intrinsic} demonstrate fine-tuning operates in low intrinsic dimension. PiSSA~\cite{meng2024pissa} initializes LoRA from principal singular components of the pre-trained weight matrix; our SVD alignment ratio measures a related but distinct quantity---the overlap of \emph{fine-tuning deltas} with the base model's principal subspace, rather than using that subspace for parameter-efficient initialization. Loss landscape visualization~\cite{li2018loss,fort2019emergent} provides complementary geometric perspectives on fine-tuning. We extend these by revealing quantitative gaps ($2$--$6\times$ for RL vs.\ $1.5\times$ for SFT) not captured by prior metrics.

\paragraph{Multi-objective RL for LLMs.}
Recent work explores multi-objective optimization in LLM alignment: Rewarded Soups~\cite{rame2024rewarded} interpolates weights fine-tuned on diverse rewards to approximate the Pareto front, MODPO~\cite{zhou2024modpo} extends DPO to handle multiple preference dimensions, and fine-grained human feedback~\cite{wu2024finegrained} decomposes rewards into per-aspect signals. These methods address \emph{training} with multiple objectives; our work addresses the complementary problem of \emph{distilling} multi-objective RL policies, where the challenge is not balancing objectives during training but transferring the teacher's coordinated multi-objective execution strategy.

\paragraph{Preference learning and DPO.}
DPO~\cite{rafailov2023dpo} and extensions (IPO~\cite{gheshlaghi2024ipo}, KTO~\cite{ethayarajh2024kto}, RAFT++~\cite{dong2023raft}) provide offline alternatives to RLHF. We use DPO as a stronger-than-SFT baseline; its failure ($+1.0$pp, not significant) suggests behavioral mismatch rather than insufficient signal richness. Following~\citet{karl2024negative}, we frame this as a diagnostic contribution.
